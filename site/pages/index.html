What it does

  • Ads, off.

    Sponsored rows, autoplaying promos, shopping tiles and Amazon's ad services are disabled. Ad tracking and usage reporting are turned off.

  • Watching you, off.

    Screen fingerprinting (ACR), watch-time tracking, telemetry and experiments are switched off.

  • Surprise updates, off.

    A small open-source DNS filter on the TV blocks Amazon's telemetry and update servers. It steps aside if Proton VPN or another VPN app is installed.

  • Your apps stay.

    Nothing you watch is removed unless you name it. Packages are disabled, never deleted.

  • Optional extras.

    Frame-rate matching for smooth 24p films, and quieter autoplay on Amazon's home screen. Both off unless you choose them.

  • Checks itself.

    A read-only health check shows memory, storage, which home screen has HOME, and anything an update switched back on.

Pick your home screen.

Both launchers stay installed. Only one catches the HOME button at a time, and you can switch whenever you like, or go back to Amazon's.

  • dontv home

    Your apps, your HDMI inputs and a clock. Rows or a grid, wallpapers, icon packs. Open source, no internet access, sells nothing.

  • Projectivy Launcher

    A popular, more customisable launcher from the Amazon Appstore. dontv hands HOME to it and keeps dontv home ready as a fallback.

  • Amazon's home

    Still there, with the ads and tracking switched off. One command brings it back.

./scripts/switch-home.sh dumbtv       # dontv home
./scripts/switch-home.sh projectivy
./scripts/switch-home.sh amazon
./scripts/switch-home.sh status       # changes nothing

One sentence, or a few commands.

Ask a coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini and others) to "clean up my Fire TV", or run the scripts yourself from macOS, Linux or Windows. Either way it asks a few questions first, then takes about ten minutes.
